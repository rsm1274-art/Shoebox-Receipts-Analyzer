Help & Manual

Receipt Audit — from your first scan to the QuickBooks export

Quick start

Receipt Audit reads a receipt, works out which QuickBooks account each line belongs to, spreads the tax and tip fairly across those accounts, checks the math, and prepares files you can bring into QuickBooks Online. Everything runs in your browser — your receipts are not uploaded anywhere.

In plain English Think of it as a very careful assistant sitting between your pile of receipts and your bookkeeping software. It reads each receipt, files every item in the right drawer (account), tells you when something looks wrong, and hands your bookkeeper a tidy list.

Your first 10 minutes

Tick each step as you go — your progress is remembered on this computer.

The golden rules

  • The app suggests; you decide. OCR (text recognition) makes mistakes. Always glance at merchant, date, amounts and accounts before exporting.
  • Keep the paper. The app keeps only a small low-resolution copy of each photo (about 60 KB) — good for a quick look, not for fine print. File the hard copies by date; the app tells you which date to look under.
  • Link a save file on day one so a browser problem can never cost you your work.
  • Anything red is a stop sign. Fix it or deliberately accept it before you export.

Upload or drag & drop

  1. In the Scan Fleet / CapEx Receipt panel, click the dashed box (or drag image files onto it).
  2. Choose one or more photos. PNG, JPG and WEBP work.
  3. Watch the progress bar. Reading takes a few seconds per receipt.
  4. The receipt appears at the top of the queue and is selected so you can check it.
PDFs are not supported. If your scanner produces PDFs, change its setting to JPG or PNG.

Using the camera

  1. Click Use Camera. Your browser asks permission to use the camera — choose Allow.
  2. If you have more than one camera, pick the right one from the menu above the picture.
  3. Lay the receipt flat, fill the frame, avoid shadows and glare, and keep the text horizontal.
  4. Click Capture. The window closes and the receipt is read.
Tips for a readable photo Daylight or a bright desk lamp beats a flash. Flatten curled thermal paper with a book. Long receipts: fold in thirds and photograph the section with the items and totals, or use a scanner instead.
Camera blocked? Click the camera icon in the address bar and set the camera to Allow, then try again. Your browser may ask again the next time you open the app. A phone cannot connect to this page directly; use a phone scan app and the watched folder instead.

Watching a scan folder (scanners, network copiers, phone scan apps)

Web pages cannot talk to a scanner directly. The reliable workaround is a folder: your scanner (or phone app) saves each scan into a folder, and Receipt Audit watches that folder and reads every new image automatically.

In plain English Set your scanner to “Scan to folder”. Then tell Receipt Audit which folder. After that, scanning a receipt is all you do — it shows up in the app by itself within a few seconds.

Set it up in the app (about 30 seconds)

  1. Click Watch Scan Folder.
  2. Choose the folder your scans arrive in, then click Select Folder and allow access. The app only reads the folder — it never moves, renames or deletes your files.
  3. If the folder already has images, you are asked: OK imports them now; Cancel ignores them and imports only new scans.
  4. The status line under the buttons shows Watching “Scans” · 3 imported · checked 2:41 PM.

Set up the scanner side

Menu names differ by brand. Look for the words below in your scanner or app.

Network scanner or copier (“Scan to folder / Scan to SMB”)
  1. Create a folder on a computer, for example C:\Receipt Scans, and share it on your network (right-click → Properties → Sharing).
  2. In the copier’s address book or web page, add a Scan to Folder / SMB destination pointing to that shared folder (computer name, share name, and a Windows user that can write to it).
  3. Set the file type to JPG (or PNG), resolution 300 dpi, one file per page, color or grayscale.
  4. Scan one receipt as a test. The file should appear in the folder.
  5. In Receipt Audit, watch that folder (on the computer where you use the app).

Your IT person or the copier’s manual covers the SMB details; the only thing Receipt Audit cares about is that image files land in a folder it can open.

Phone scan app (Microsoft Lens, Adobe Scan, Google Drive scan, etc.)
  1. Install a scan app that can save images to a cloud drive (OneDrive, Google Drive, Dropbox, iCloud Drive).
  2. On your PC, install that drive’s desktop app so the cloud folder is synced to a normal folder (for example OneDrive\Receipt Scans).
  3. In the phone app, choose Save as image (JPG), not PDF, and save into that cloud folder.
  4. In Receipt Audit, watch the synced folder. Receipts photographed on your phone appear a few moments after they sync.
A scanner plugged into this PC (flatbed or sheet-fed)
  1. Open the scanner’s own software (for example Windows Scan, or the brand’s utility).
  2. Set File type: JPG (or PNG), 300 dpi, and a Save to folder such as C:\Receipt Scans.
  3. Scan. Then watch that folder in Receipt Audit.

Good to know

  • New files are read after they stop changing for about 2½ seconds, so a half-written scan is not grabbed too early. The folder is checked about every 4 seconds.
  • The app remembers which files it has read (by name, size and date), so nothing is imported twice, even after you close and reopen it.
  • PDFs in the folder are skipped and counted in the status line.
  • Large photos are shrunk to a sensible size before reading. This speeds things up and does not change your original files.
  • Stop Watching pauses it. After you restart the browser, Chrome may need your OK again — the button then says Resume Watching.
  • Only one folder can be watched at a time.
Chrome or Edge only. Firefox and Safari cannot give a web page ongoing access to a folder. Use upload there.

What happens after a scan

  1. Read. The picture is turned into text (OCR).
  2. Parse. The app looks for a merchant name, a date, a card type, item lines with prices, and Subtotal / Tax / Tip / Total lines.
  3. Categorize. Each item is matched to an account using keywords (see Accounts & categories).
  4. Split. Tax and tip are shared across the accounts in proportion to the items (see Splits).
  5. Audit. The math and policy checks run (see Audit flags).
Check these every time. When the app cannot recognize something it makes a guess: the date defaults to today, the card defaults to the first active Paid From account (the Chase Ink Visa *4921 unless you changed the list), and the van defaults to the first active class (Van #1 unless changed). If your receipt is a different card or van, change it in the Inspector.

Scan-quality checklist

  • One receipt per image; the whole receipt in frame, edges visible.
  • Text upright and sharp — zoom in on the picture; if you cannot read it, neither can the app.
  • Even lighting, no glare, no shadows across the text.
  • 300 dpi for scanners; for cameras, hold steady about 30 cm (1 ft) away.
  • Faded thermal paper: scan it the day you get it, in color or grayscale, and bump the brightness if your scanner allows.

Reviewing & correcting a receipt

Click a receipt in the queue and the Inspector fills in. Work through it top to bottom.

In plain English The app made its best guess. Your job is to read the receipt next to the screen and fix anything that does not match. Orange or red bars point at the likeliest problems.

Header: engine, date, memo

The top shows how the receipt was read, the date as QuickBooks will see it (MM/DD/YYYY), any fuel details found, the merchant name, and the QBO Memo Preview — the exact description that goes into the bank-feed and .QBO files.

Fix the merchant and date right here. Click the merchant name to retype it, and use the date box to pick the correct day. The memo, the QBO date shown next to it, the “hard copy filed under” note and every export update straight away. An empty merchant or an invalid date is refused.

The three routing menus

  • Paid From QBO Account — which card or bank account paid. Pick the one that matches the receipt. Choose Founder Personal Card when the owner paid personally so it lands as reimbursable.
  • Fleet / Van Assignment (QBO Class) — which vehicle the cost belongs to. Use General Fleet / Shared Ops for costs shared across vans.
  • Expense Phase Tag — Operating (Recurring) for normal running costs, CapEx (One-Off / Sec. 195) for launch and equipment purchases. It is set automatically when the receipt is first scanned but does not change if you later re-categorize lines — change it yourself if needed.

Tax-inclusive split breakdown

The pills show how the receipt will post to QuickBooks: one pill per account, each with its tax-inclusive amount. Split Sum should equal the card charge. If it does not, something on the receipt does not add up — check the audit flags.

Line items & accounts

  • Description — click to edit. Changing the words re-runs the keyword matching for that line (unless you picked its account by hand).
  • QBO Account — choose any active account. A hand-picked account is locked: edits to the description will not override it.
  • Qty — how many. Scanned lines start at 1. Edit it and the line becomes quantity × unit price in every split, flag and export.
  • Unit $ — the pre-tax price of one unit. Edit it and every split updates.
  • QBO Split $ — that line’s price plus its share of tax and tip. (Hover for the explanation.)
  • + Add Line Item — adds a $0.00 line you can describe and price, for something OCR missed.
  • × (delete line) — removes a junk or double-scanned line after a confirmation. The Extracted Subtotal box is not recalculated for you: after deleting, check it against the paper. If the lines no longer add up to it, a red flag tells you.

Totals

Four boxes you can edit: Extracted Subtotal, Sales Tax, Tip / Gratuity and Receipt Printed Total. Make them match the paper receipt. The checks compare them to your line items and to each other, and tax and tip drive the split amounts.

The quickest fix for a math flag Compare the printed total on the paper with the Printed Total box. If OCR misread a digit, correct it and the red flag clears.

Photo area & raw OCR text

Every scanned receipt keeps a small low-resolution copy (about 60 KB, black-and-white) with your saved work. Receipts scanned in this session show the full photo; after you close and reopen the app they show the saved copy with a note. Click the picture to enlarge it (click again or press Esc to close). It is a reference, not a substitute: small print may be hard to read, so pull the hard copy filed under the receipt’s date when you need it.

Receipts without a saved copy (for example the built-in samples before a copy is made) show “No photo copy stored — pull the hard copy filed under <date>” with the merchant and total. Open View Raw Local OCR Output to see exactly what the reader saw — useful when a line looks odd.

Deleting a receipt

The trash button removes the receipt from the queue and from the saved session, after a confirmation. It cannot be undone, but a save file you made earlier still contains it.

Clear all (above the receipt list) removes every receipt at once. It shows a warning with the number of receipts, reminds you to export and make an archive copy, tells you whether a save file is linked (and offers a backup download), and only enables the button after you type CLEAR. Your account list and settings are kept. For a short time afterwards (until you scan another receipt, or close or reload the page) an Undo clear link brings the receipts back.

Audit flags explained

Flags appear in the Fleet & CapEx Policy Audit section of the Inspector. Each has a colour (severity) and a short code.

HighMoney or tax treatment is at risk. Resolve before exporting.
MediumWorth a look; often legitimate (a split receipt, a generous tip).
LowMissing detail for record-keeping.
GreenAll checks passed.

Every flag, what triggers it, and what to do

High TOTAL_MATH_ERROR — Receipt total math discrepancy

Trigger: Subtotal + Tax + Tip differs from the Printed Total by more than 5¢.

Usually means: OCR misread a number, or the receipt has a fee/discount line the app did not catch.

Do: Compare with the paper; correct the wrong box; or add a line for the missing fee/discount. If the paper itself is wrong, keep the receipt, export without it (see Export), and raise it with the merchant.

High SUBTOTAL_MISMATCH — Line item sum ≠ subtotal

Trigger: The line items add to something more than 5¢ away from the Subtotal box.

Usually means: A line was missed or misread, or quantities were ignored.

Do: Add the missing line or fix the price. Note each line is treated as quantity 1 on scanned receipts, so a “2 × $9.00” line printed as one total should be entered at its total price.

High OWNER_DRAW_SPLIT — Personal / non-deductible item

Trigger: An item was filed to Owner’s Draw / Personal, or its name contains “wine”, “cabernet” or “gift card”.

Means: That amount is kept out of business expenses so it does not inflate deductions.

Do: If the item really was personal, leave it. If it was a legitimate business purchase (for example a client gift card), choose a business account for that line.

High CAPEX_THRESHOLD — Fixed asset / CapEx threshold alert

Trigger: A single line (price × quantity, before tax) is $2,500 or more.

Means: The line is filed to Fixed Assets: Equipment & Tools (> $2,500) instead of an expense account, because large purchases are generally recorded as assets and depreciated rather than expensed at once.

Do: Keep it as a fixed asset and tell your accountant, or override the account if your accountant says otherwise. Whether a purchase can be expensed immediately depends on your tax elections — that is your CPA’s decision, not the app’s.

Medium MIXED_RECEIPT_SPLIT — Split transaction required

Trigger: The items on one receipt map to more than one account.

Means: One card charge will be booked in several pieces. Tax and tip have been shared across the pieces for you.

Do: Just check the pieces look right. In QuickBooks, enter or accept it as a split transaction.

Medium EXCESSIVE_TIP — High gratuity

Trigger: Tip is more than 20.5% of the subtotal.

Do: Confirm the tip. Tips above your company meal policy may need approval or a note.

Medium DUPLICATE_ITEM — Double-scanned item

Trigger: Two or more lines share the same description and the same price.

Do: If the receipt really has two, ignore it. If it is a double read, set one line’s price to 0.00 so the totals match.

Low FUEL_GALLONS_CHECK — Fuel receipt missing gallons

Trigger: A line was filed to a Fuel account but no “21.500 GAL” style text was found.

Do: If you track mileage or fuel tax (for example IFTA), note the gallons from the paper receipt. The flag does not block anything.

For accountants Thresholds are fixed in the code: math tolerance $0.05, excessive tip > 20.5%, capitalization line threshold ≥ $2,500 pre-tax per line. The $2,500 flag is raised from the line amount regardless of which account the line is ultimately assigned to, and independent of manual overrides. Flags are recomputed from scratch every time a receipt is edited; there is no record of past flags or of who accepted them.

Accounts & categories

The QBO Accounts window is your chart of accounts inside the app: the list of “drawers” that receipt lines can be filed into. The names should match the account names in QuickBooks.

In plain English A “category” or “account” is a label like Vehicle: Fuel & DEF. Each item on a receipt gets one. Keywords are the clues the app uses to pick the label automatically — if an item says “diesel”, it goes under Fuel.

Two tiers

  • Tier 1 — Core Operating (permanent). Everyday running costs: fuel, maintenance, tolls, supplies, meals, office, personal items.
  • Tier 2 — CapEx & Launch (archivable). One-off start-up and equipment accounts. These can be hidden when you are past the launch phase (see CapEx mode).

How to add a new account (category)

  1. Click QBO Accounts in the top bar.
  2. In Add Custom QBO Account Category, type the name exactly as it appears in QuickBooks, for example CapEx: Warehouse Security. For a sub-account use the Parent: Sub form QuickBooks shows.
  3. Choose the tier: Tier 1 for permanent running costs, Tier 2 for CapEx / launch items.
  4. Type keywords, separated by commas, that appear on receipt lines for this account, for example padlock, camera, cctv, alarm. Leave it blank if you only want to pick this account by hand.
  5. Click Add QBO Account. It appears at the bottom of its tier list and in every line-item account menu immediately.
  6. Create the same account in QuickBooks (Chart of Accounts) so the names match when you import.
New keywords are not retroactive. They apply to receipts scanned afterwards and to any receipt you edit. Receipts already in the queue are not re-sorted until you change something on them.

Writing good keywords

  • Use words that appear on the receipt line, not your own name for the account.
  • Matching ignores capitals and looks for the keyword anywhere inside the line’s words. tire also matches “entire”; paper also matches “sandpaper”.
  • Longer, more specific keywords are safer: oil change beats oil.
  • Merchant names work well on fuel receipts (shell, pilot).
  • The list is checked top to bottom, and the first account with a matching keyword wins. The built-in accounts come first, then the ones you add, in the order you added them.
Common surprises The built-in keywords service and wash (Repairs & Maintenance) and water (Meals) are broad — a “service fee” line files to Maintenance and bottled water files to Meals. Fix such a line with its account menu; the choice is then locked for that line.

Archive, restore, rename, delete

  • Archive hides an account from the menus (struck through in the list) and stops it auto-matching new lines. Restore brings it back. Lines already filed there keep their account and show “(Archived)”.
  • Rename: retype the name in the account’s box and press Enter or click away. Every receipt line already filed there moves to the new name and the splits and exports are rebuilt, so old and new receipts stay consistent. Two accounts cannot share a name.
  • Keywords: edit the comma-separated words in the account’s second box. Wrap a word in “double quotes” to keep its spaces (the built-in "def " needs its trailing space so it does not match “default”). After a change the app offers to re-check receipts already in the queue — lines whose account you picked by hand are never touched.
  • Delete: accounts are never deleted — only archived — so old receipts always make sense.

Adding a card, bank account or van

Open QBO Accounts and scroll below the two account tiers: the Paid From accounts (cards and bank accounts) and the Fleet / Van classes each have their own list. You can add, rename and archive entries, exactly like accounts. Today they are:

  • Paid From words: each card or bank account has a “words” box (for example 4921, amex). When a receipt is scanned, the app looks for those words, or the last four card digits, in the payment text it read, and the first active account that matches (in list order) is chosen. If none matches, the first active account is used. Add the last four digits of a new card so scans find it.
  • Renaming an entry also renames it on every receipt that uses it. Archiving removes it from the menus and from automatic matching; receipts already using it keep it and show “(Archived)”. At least one entry of each list must stay active.
  • New receipts start in the first active Fleet / Van class when the class is not known.
For accountants
  • Precedence for each non-locked line: (1) price × quantity ≥ $2,500 → Fixed Assets: Equipment & Tools (> $2,500); (2) first account in list order whose keyword is a case-insensitive substring of the description; (3) the line’s existing account, else Operating Supplies & Moving Gear. A manual selection sets a lock that bypasses all three.
  • Keyword matching reads only active accounts: archived accounts and Tier 2 accounts hidden by CapEx Mode OFF do not auto-match (they used to). Lines already filed to such an account keep it until you change them.
  • Built-in rules follow the account identity, not its text, so renaming is safe: the $2,500 rule files to the Fixed Assets account under whatever name it now has, and the personal-item and fuel-gallons checks still recognise the Owner’s Draw and Fuel accounts. Renaming rewrites the category text on existing lines and rebuilds their splits.
  • Account identity is the display name (text). Exports carry the name, not an ID, so a name that differs from QuickBooks will not map automatically.
  • The 50% limit on Meals (50% Limit) & Driver Per Diem is a label only — the app does not compute or apply any limitation.

CapEx mode & fixed assets

In plain English “CapEx” (capital expenditure) means buying something that lasts for years — shelving for the van, a liftgate — rather than something you use up, like fuel. Accountants often record these differently from everyday expenses. The app separates them so your accountant can see them at a glance.

The CapEx Mode button

  • CapEx Mode: ON — Tier 2 accounts (CapEx & launch) are available in the account menus. Use this while you are setting up the business.
  • CapEx Mode: OFF (Year 2+) — Tier 2 accounts are hidden from the menus so day-to-day coding stays simple. Lines already filed to them keep their account (shown with “(Archived)”).

How CapEx shows up in the app

  • The CapEx Spend total adds every split that is filed to a Tier 2 / CapEx / Fixed Asset account, or belongs to a receipt whose Expense Phase Tag is CapEx (One-Off).
  • The queue filter CapEx Only shows receipts tagged CapEx or containing a CapEx / Fixed Asset split.
  • Lines of $2,500 or more are filed to Fixed Assets: Equipment & Tools (> $2,500) and flagged (CAPEX_THRESHOLD).
  • Exports include the receipt’s phase so your accountant can filter on it.
Tag the whole receipt carefully. If a receipt is tagged CapEx (One-Off), all of its splits count toward the CapEx Spend total — even ordinary-expense lines on the same receipt. For a mixed receipt, tag it by its main purpose, or rely on the account of each line.
For accountants The app tags and totals; it makes no tax determinations. Labels such as “Sec. 195”, “De Minimis Safe Harbor” and “Section 179 / Depreciation” are prompts for your review. Applicability of start-up cost treatment, the de minimis safe harbor election, expensing elections and depreciation methods depends on the entity’s facts and elections and must be decided by the preparer. The $2,500 line threshold is a fixed constant, applied per line before tax, not per invoice.

Splits, tax & tip

In plain English If one receipt has fuel and a toll and a snack, QuickBooks needs each piece filed separately — but you only paid once. The app divides the sales tax and tip so every piece carries its fair share and the pieces add back up to exactly what your card was charged.

QuickBooks wants splits to add up to the exact card charge, so the app includes each account’s share of tax and tip in its amount. That is what the QBO Split $ column and the split pills show.

The rule

Split for an account = its items’ subtotal + (Tax + Tip) × (its items’ subtotal ÷ all items’ subtotal)

The last account absorbs any rounding pennies so the total matches the printed total exactly (as long as the receipt’s own math is within 5¢).

Try it

Change the numbers and see the split. This runs the app’s real calculation.

ItemPrice $Account
For accountants
  • Allocation base is the sum of line items (price × quantity), not the printed subtotal. Tax and tip are allocated together with one ratio.
  • Each split is rounded to cents; allocated tax/tip is unrounded until the split amount is rounded.
  • The rounding plug goes to the last split in the order the accounts first appear on the receipt, and only when |(items + tax + tip) − printed total| < $0.05. Otherwise splits are the plain rounded values and may not sum to the printed total (this is why such receipts carry a math flag).
  • If line items sum to $0 the shares are equal.
  • Tax is not separated into a sales-tax liability or use-tax account; it is embedded in each split’s amount.

  2. When asked, pick the QuickBooks account the transactions belong to.
  3. Review and categorize as in Option 1.

For accountants
  • Option 1: columns Date (MM/DD/YYYY), Description (quoted), Credit (always empty), Debit (receipt total, 2 dp). Totals use the Printed Total field, not the sum of splits.
  • Option 2 file: the CapEx_Flag column is YES or NO, the same as the on-screen preview (YES when the receipt’s phase tag is CapEx). Amounts are plain numbers (no $). All text cells are double-quote escaped.
  • Option 3 (OFX 1.02 SGML): one CCSTMTRS with ACCTID RECEIPTAUDIT-FLEET-4921, TRNTYPE DEBIT, TRNAMT negative total, DTPOSTED = receipt date 12:00:00, FITID = receipt ID, NAME max 32 chars, MEMO max 240 chars, INTU.BID 3000, DTSTART fixed 20260101. Because the account ID is constant, QuickBooks may associate every import with a single account.
  • Receipt IDs (and therefore FITIDs) are generated at scan time. Re-scanning the same paper creates a new ID, so re-scans are not deduplicated.
  • Dates that were not ISO (YYYY-MM-DD) in the OCR text are written to CSVs as read. Verify any receipt whose date format looks unusual.
  • Excluding math-error receipts removes them from all three files and from the preview.

Saving & backups

In plain English The app saves for you after every change — you never press “Save”. For safety it keeps a second copy in a file you choose. If the browser crashes, just reopen the app and carry on.

Two layers of protection

  1. Autosave in the browser. Every change is saved within about half a second. Reopen the app and your last session is restored.
  2. A save file on disk (recommended). Click Save File once and pick a place such as Documents\Receipt Audit. From then on the file is updated automatically.

What is saved — and what is not

  • Saved: every receipt’s extracted data and edits, the raw OCR text, your chart of accounts (including added, renamed and re-keyworded accounts and archive status), your Paid From accounts and Fleet / Van classes, your selected receipt, and the CapEx Mode setting.
  • Saved: a small low-resolution copy of each receipt photo (about 60 KB, black-and-white), kept in the browser and inside your save file so it travels with your work.
  • Not saved: the full-size photo. Keep the paper (or the original scan files) filed by date. A restored receipt tells you which date to look under.

Moving, restoring and backing up

  • Open loads a session file and continues saving to it.
  • New computer or browser: copy the save file across, click Open.
  • Backups: include the save file in your normal backup (or a cloud-synced folder). It is a single .json file.
  • How big does it get? Each receipt adds roughly 75 KB to the file, so about 4 MB for a month of 50 receipts and about 45 MB for a year. Saving stays quick: the picture copies are stored once when you scan, ordinary edits never rewrite them in your browser, and the save file is refreshed a few seconds after you stop making changes (or instantly when you click the status chip, link or open a file, or close the tab).
  • If a save file is newer than the browser’s copy, the app uses the newer one; when reconnecting it asks before overwriting a newer file.
  • Reset 4 Fleet Samples, Delete and Clear all ask for confirmation because changes are permanent (Clear all also asks you to type CLEAR).
Privacy. A save file contains your receipt data (merchants, dates, amounts, items) in plain text and small pictures of your receipts. Store it like you would your books, and do not leave it in a shared or public folder.

Closing out a year (year-end & starting a new year)

In plain English At the end of the year you put this year’s receipts in a labelled “box” (an archive file) and start over for next year with a clean, light file. Nothing is lost: the archive keeps everything, and you can look inside it any time.

Do this once a year (or whenever the save file gets heavy). It takes about ten minutes. The app has no “close year” button — you do it with the buttons you already know, in this order. Do not skip the order: export and archive first, delete last.

  1. Finish the year’s review. Filter the queue to Audit Flagged Only and clear or knowingly accept every red flag. Check every receipt’s merchant, date, amounts and accounts against the paper.
  2. Export everything you need. Open QBO Export Suite and download the file(s) you use (see Exporting to QuickBooks). The exports include every receipt currently in the queue, so do this before you delete anything. The file names carry today’s date.
  3. Confirm the paper is filed. The app only keeps small low-resolution pictures, so make sure the hard copies for the year are filed by date.
  4. Make the archive. Wait until the status chip shows Saved… with your file name, then click Save File and give it a clear name such as receipt-audit-2026-ARCHIVE.json in a safe folder. This writes the whole year to that file.
  5. Start next year’s file. Click Save File again and name it for the new year, for example receipt-audit-2027.json. From now on the app saves to this new file, and the archive is no longer touched.
    Check the chip It should now show the new file name. If it still shows the archive name, repeat this step — otherwise the deletions in the next step would change your archive.
  6. Clear the closed-out receipts. To empty the whole queue, click Clear all above the receipt list. A warning shows how many receipts will go, reminds you to export and archive, and asks you to type CLEAR before the button works.
    To keep a few receipts that still belong to unfinished business, delete the others one at a time with the trash button in the Inspector instead.
    Changed your mind? Right after clearing, an Undo clear link appears where the button was. It works until you scan another receipt or close or reload the page.
  7. Tidy the accounts (optional). Past the launch phase? Switch CapEx Mode to OFF (Year 2+) and archive launch-only accounts in QBO Accounts. Your account list carries into the new file as it is. Ask your accountant whether it is time.
  8. Confirm. The status chip should read Saved · receipt-audit-2027.json, and the queue should show only what you meant to keep.

Looking something up in an archive later

  1. Wait for the chip to show your current file as Saved.
  2. Click Open and choose the archive file (confirm the replace). Best practice: open a copy of the archive, because opening a file also makes the app save back into it.
  3. Look around. Avoid editing unless you mean to.
  4. To go back, click Open again and choose the current-year file.
Store the archive like your books Keep the archive file, the dated exports and the filed paper receipts together, and keep one extra copy somewhere else (external drive or cloud storage). The archive contains small pictures of your receipts, so keep it private.
For accountants Year-end is a manual copy-and-clear process, not a ledger close: there is no period lock, so the archive is a point-in-time file, not a frozen record. For an evidentiary trail keep the dated exports, the paper originals, and a read-only copy of the archive (and note its modified date). Exports contain every loaded receipt regardless of date, so to produce a single-period export, load only that period’s receipts (for example by opening that period’s file) before exporting. The chart of accounts and CapEx Mode carry across files unchanged.
For accountants Session file: JSON, schema 1 (app: receipt-audit), containing receipts, coaAccounts, selectedReceiptId, startupPhaseMode and savedAt, plus a top-level thumbs map of receipt ID → JPEG data URL (grayscale, targeted at ≤ 60 KB, long edge up to 1,000 px). The images are reference copies only and are not evidence-grade; the file text is rewritten in full after an idle delay of about 4 seconds (at most 30 seconds behind), while the browser copy is updated within half a second. There is no audit trail: edits overwrite prior values, nothing records who changed what, and deleted receipts are gone from the live session. For evidentiary retention rely on the original paper/scan files and dated exports. Browser storage is shared by every copy of the app opened from the same location, so the three look-and-feel variants share one session.

Reading engines (OCR)

In plain English The “engine” is the part that reads the words on the picture. The default (WASM OCR) works out of the box. You can ignore the other one.
  • WASM OCR — reads the picture inside your browser using the Tesseract engine. No setup. Best on clear, flat, high-contrast receipts.
  • Ollama VLM — asks a vision AI model running on your own computer to read the receipt. Often better on messy or curved receipts, but needs a capable PC and some setup.

Setting up Ollama (optional)

  1. Install Ollama from ollama.com and download a vision model:
    ollama pull llama3.2-vision
  2. Start it so web pages are allowed to talk to it (Windows PowerShell):
    $env:OLLAMA_ORIGINS="*"; ollama serve

    If Ollama already runs in the background, quit it from the system tray first.

  3. In the app switch to Ollama VLM. A panel appears with the address (http://localhost:11434) and model name. Click Ping Localhost — the badge should turn green.
  4. Scan as usual.

“100% local” — what it means

Your receipt images and data are processed on your computer and never sent to a server. The page does fetch its reading software, icons and fonts from the internet when it opens, so a first launch needs an internet connection.

Glossary

Plain-language definitions, from everyday words to accounting terms.

Receipt Queue
The list of receipts currently loaded in the app.
Inspector
The right-hand panel that shows and lets you edit one receipt.
OCR (optical character recognition)
Software that turns a picture of text into text a computer can use. It is good but not perfect.
Chart of accounts (CoA)
The list of accounts your books use to sort income and expenses. Here: the QBO Accounts window.
Account / category
One entry in the chart of accounts, for example Vehicle: Fuel & DEF. The app uses the two words interchangeably.
Tier 1 / Tier 2
Tier 1 = permanent day-to-day accounts. Tier 2 = CapEx and launch accounts you can hide later.
Keyword
A word the app looks for in a receipt line to choose its account automatically.
Split (split transaction)
One payment recorded across several accounts.
Pro-rata
Shared out in proportion. Tax and tip are shared among accounts in proportion to each account’s items.
Tax-inclusive amount
An amount that already includes its share of sales tax (and tip).
CapEx (capital expenditure)
Spending on assets that last more than a year (equipment, vehicle upfitting) as opposed to everyday expenses.
Fixed asset
A long-lived item the business owns, usually recorded on the balance sheet and written off over time (depreciation).
De minimis safe harbor
A U.S. tax rule that lets some businesses expense small purchases (up to a per-item/invoice limit) instead of capitalizing them. The app uses $2,500 as its alert level; whether it applies to you is a question for your CPA.
Section 195 (start-up costs)
U.S. tax rules about costs incurred to start a business. The app tags such spending as CapEx / launch; the tax treatment is for your CPA.
Section 179 / depreciation
Ways of deducting the cost of business equipment, all at once or over several years. Mentioned in a flag as a prompt for review.
Owner’s draw
Money or items taken by the owner for personal use. Not a business expense.
Per diem
A daily allowance for travel or meal costs.
QBO Class
A QuickBooks tag used to track spending by something other than account — here, which van.
Paid From account
The bank, card or cash account the money came out of.
Reimbursable (due to owner)
A business cost the owner paid personally and should be repaid.
Bank feed
Transactions flowing into QuickBooks from a bank or card, waiting to be categorized.
CSV
A plain table file that opens in Excel or Google Sheets.
OFX / .QBO (Web Connect)
A bank-statement file format QuickBooks can import.
FITID
The unique ID on each transaction in an OFX/.QBO file. QuickBooks uses it to avoid importing the same transaction twice.
Memo / description
The text note attached to a transaction.
Audit flag
A warning the app raises about a receipt. Colour shows severity.
IFTA
International Fuel Tax Agreement — fuel tax reporting for interstate trucking. Why gallons are recorded.
Session file
The save file holding your receipts and accounts.
Autosave
The app saving by itself after every change.
Hard copy
The paper receipt. The app keeps only a small low-resolution picture of it — keep the paper filed by date.

Troubleshooting & FAQ

I closed the browser. Is my work gone?

No. Reopen the app and it restores your last session automatically (“Restored N receipts”). If you linked a save file you also have that copy. Each receipt’s small low-resolution picture is kept too; only the full-size photo is not.

The status chip is red and says “Reconnect save file”

Your browser asks again after a restart. Click the chip and choose Allow. Your work was still saved in the browser meanwhile.

The merchant or date is wrong

Fix it in the Inspector: click the merchant name to retype it, and pick the right day in the date box. Exports and the memo update immediately. The raw OCR text shows what the reader saw.

The numbers are wrong or the red math flag will not clear

Compare the four boxes at the bottom of the Inspector (Subtotal, Tax, Tip, Printed Total) with the paper. Correct whichever is misread. If the lines do not add up to the subtotal, add the missing line, or set a duplicate line to 0.00.

An item went into the wrong account

Choose the right one from that line’s account menu — the choice is locked. To make the app get it right next time, add a keyword to the right account (see Accounts & categories). Remember the first matching account in the list wins.

I can’t find an account I need

Check CapEx Mode — Tier 2 accounts are hidden when it is OFF. Check whether the account is archived (QBO Accounts → Restore). If it truly does not exist, add it.

The camera does not start

Allow camera access (camera icon in the address bar), close other programs that use the camera (video calls), and try again. If you have several cameras, choose another in the menu.

Scan folder isn’t importing anything
  • The status line should say Watching. If it says Paused, click Resume Watching.
  • Are the files JPG/PNG/WEBP/BMP/GIF? PDFs are skipped (the status line counts them).
  • Files that were already in the folder when you started are ignored if you chose Cancel at the prompt.
  • A file still being written is picked up a few seconds later.
  • Make sure your scanner is saving to the folder you chose (open it in File Explorer).
The saved picture of a receipt is too small or blurry to read

That is by design — the app keeps a compact copy (about 60 KB) so the save file stays small and fast. Click the picture to enlarge it, and use the paper receipt filed under the same date when you need the fine print.

The save file is getting large, or saving feels slow

Expect roughly 4 MB per month at 50 receipts a month. The status chip may say “updating file…” for a few seconds after a change; that is normal. If the file lives in a synced folder (OneDrive, Dropbox), a large file re-uploads each time it changes — keeping it in a local folder and copying it to the cloud as a backup works better. At year-end, follow Closing out a year: archive the old file, link a new one named for the new year, and clear the closed-out receipts.

Reading is slow or stuck

Large photos take longer. Try a smaller/clearer picture. The first read after opening the app also has to load the reading software from the internet.

Export buttons do nothing

There must be at least one receipt in the queue (and not all excluded by the math-error checkbox). Your browser may also have blocked the download — check its downloads bar or address bar.

QuickBooks says the file is wrong or the columns do not match

Use Option 1 for QuickBooks’ bank upload. Option 2 is a ledger, not a QuickBooks template. If the .QBO file is rejected, switch to Option 1.

Why did a line go to “Fixed Assets”?

Any single line of $2,500 or more (before tax) is filed there automatically. If your accountant says it should be expensed, change that line’s account.

Which browsers work?

Chrome and Edge are fully supported. Scan-folder watching and live save-file updates need Chrome or Edge. Other browsers can use upload, with a manual download for backups.

It’s the end of the year — how do I archive and start over?

Export, save an archive copy of the save file, link a new file for the new year, then clear the old receipts. The full checklist is in Closing out a year (Saving & backups).

How do I start completely fresh?

For a new year see Closing out a year. Otherwise: use Clear all above the queue (or delete receipts one at a time). To reset the accounts, you would need to clear this site’s stored data in the browser settings — do this only after making a backup with Save File.

For accountants: controls & limitations

What the app is

A local, single-user receipt-capture and coding aid. It produces proposed coding and a transaction file. It is not a ledger, has no approvals, no user identities and no change history.

Data per receipt

ID, merchant, date, payment text, Paid-From account, class (van), expense phase, subtotal, tax, tip, printed total, line items (name, qty, price, account, lock flag, tax-inclusive amount), computed splits, audit flags, fuel metadata, raw OCR text, engine label.

Recommended review procedure

  1. Filter the queue to Audit Flagged Only; clear or accept every High flag with a note outside the app.
  2. For each receipt confirm merchant/date against the paper (both are editable in the Inspector) and confirm the Paid-From account (default is the first active Paid-From account, the Chase Visa *4921 unless changed, when the card is not recognised).
  3. Review the CapEx and Fixed Asset population; confirm tax treatment and elections.
  4. Reconcile export: Option 2 split totals per ReceiptRefID equal the receipt total; Option 1/3 totals equal the printed totals.
  5. Retain the hard copies and the dated exports as the audit record.

Known limitations

  • OCR accuracy depends on the image; the parser expects “description ... price” lines and labelled Subtotal/Tax/Tip/Total lines.
  • Scanned lines start at quantity 1; quantity is editable and multiplies unit price in all calculations. Deleting a line does not recalculate the stored Extracted Subtotal — the subtotal-mismatch flag fires until it is corrected.
  • There is no edit history: changes to merchant, date, quantities, deleted lines and renamed accounts overwrite the previous value (keep dated session files if you need a trail).
  • Accounts, Paid From accounts and classes can be added, renamed and archived but never deleted. Their identity is the display name; exports carry the name, so rename them to match QuickBooks.
  • The expense phase is assigned at scan time and is not recalculated when lines are re-coded.
  • No duplicate-receipt detection across receipts (only duplicate lines within one receipt).
  • No multi-currency support; amounts are treated as USD.
  • Single-user, single-browser storage; no concurrency control between two open windows.
  • The .QBO export uses a fixed account ID and a fixed statement start date.
